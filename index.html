Made for This

Ephesians 2:1–10

We didn't earn our place in God's family. He made us alive when we were dead, and He did it as a gift. So when we serve His house on Sunday, we're not working to earn anything. We're walking in the good works He set up for us before we were born.

“For we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus for good works, which God prepared before that we would walk in them.”Ephesians 2:10

This post isn't an accident. Walk in it.

Read the full devotional

Key points

1. Serve from thanks, not to earn.We were saved by grace, not by works. We don't serve so God will love us. We serve because He already does. That keeps our hearts right, even on a long Sunday.

2. The real fight is unseen.Pastor Philip reminded us the enemy works everywhere, from big systems down to arguments between people. Watch the doors, and also watch for division, gossip, and pride on our own team.

3. Your assignment was prepared.God planned good works for us ahead of time. The family you greet, the child you keep safe, the moment you step in: these aren't random. Ask Him each week, "What would you have me to do?"

A prayer for us

Father, thank You for making us alive when we were dead. Thank You that our salvation is a gift we could never earn. Help us serve Your house with grateful hearts, not proud ones. Open our eyes to the work You prepared for us, and keep us alert to protect Your people. In Jesus' name, amen.

    The key steps from each SOP in the AB Playbook. Tap one to open it. After any incident: file an incident report and debrief with the team.

    Watch & Locate

    BOLO ProtocolMatch the threat level, then act. AB traffic on Channel 9.
    1
    Low. Not considered dangerous; information only.
    Observe and report. Don't engage without backup or verification.
    2
    Moderate. Possible non-violent crime, no known violent history. May flee or resist.
    Approach with caution; detain with backup if necessary.
    3
    High. Potentially armed, dangerous, or violent history.
    Do not approach alone. Treat as high-risk.
    4
    Critical. Immediate, extreme danger (active shooter, violent person).
    Broadcast to all units immediately. Initiate lockdown or alerts.
    Missing ChildAlert, lock the exits, search by zone. Not found in 10 minutes: call 911.
    Not found within 10 minutes → call 911.

    1 · Respond immediately

    • Alert the nearest staff member and the Child Safety Coordinator.
    • Secure the area. No one leaves the premises until further notice.
    • Get the details: full name, age, description (height, weight, hair, clothing), last place and time seen, special needs. Get the parent's contact info.

    2 · Search

    • Split into search teams by zone: classrooms, restrooms, playgrounds, parking, common areas.
    • Search every area systematically. Stay in touch by radio.
    • Put someone on every exit with the child's description.

    3 · Communicate

    • Keep the parent informed of every step.
    • Be discreet with the congregation. Keep people calm.

    4 · After

    • Confirm the child's identity, reunite with the parent, and do a quick wellness check.
    • Incident report and debrief.

    Signs

    • Sudden emotional outbursts.
    • Screaming, hissing, or mocking.
    • Collapse without a medical cause.
    • Disruption during altar call or worship.

    Response

    • Stay calm. Don't escalate, provoke, or label anyone.
    • Discreetly notify the prayer team (SRL) or a designated pastor or intercessor.
    • If it's unsafe or disruptive, approach with two team members: “We're here to help. Let's step out and pray with you.”

    • Pray together afterward.

    Fire EmergencyPull the alarm, call 911, clear the building, headcount. No re-entry.

    1 · Respond immediately

    • Activate the fire alarm.
    • Notify the Fire Safety Coordinator.
    • Call 911 and give the church address: 3350 Greenbriar Pkwy SW.
    • Use an extinguisher only if the fire is small and you're trained.

    2 · Evacuate

    • Announce the evacuation clearly and calmly.
    • Use the marked routes to the assembly area. No elevators.
    • Check every room, including restrooms and storage.
    • Help anyone with mobility challenges.
    • Do a headcount at the assembly area.

    3 · Communicate

    • Tell the fire department who may still be inside and where.
    • Keep leadership and the congregation updated.
    No one goes back in until the fire department clears it.
    Medical EmergencyScene safe, call for help, care if trained. Don't move them.

    1 · Respond immediately

    • Figure out what's happening and make sure the scene is safe.
    • Alert the nearest staff member, medical volunteer, or First Aid Coordinator.
    • Call 911 if it's life-threatening or beyond what we can handle.
    • First aid, CPR, or AED if you're trained and it's safe.
    Don't move the person unless they're in immediate danger.

    2 · Communicate

    • Contact their emergency contact if known.
    • Keep leadership informed. Be discreet with the congregation.

    3 · When EMS arrives

    • Guide responders to the person.
    • Share any medical history you know.
    • Clear space around them so responders can work.
    Active ShooterAct immediately. Whoever is closest has the right of way.
    All steps happen immediately. Whoever is closest to the threat has the right of way.
    • Firearm at low-ready. Only fire with a clear shot and no people in the way.
    • Command people near you to get down and take cover. Be loud and firm.
    • If the threat is outside the room, lock down and keep people in place.
    • AB1 and AB2 cover the pastor and first family, move them to the worship team staging area, and lock down.
    • Roamers move to the threat. Only give AB1 and AB2 the call to move (extraction or greenroom) once you can see it's handled.
    • Listen to your radio for direction.
    Know your post's assignment. The full position-by-position steps are in the AB Playbook.

    Disruptive people

    From the AB Playbook, the Sheepdog Safety Member study guide, and the Sheepdog Safety Drills Manual.

    When is someone considered disruptive?

    It's about behavior, not who the person is. Someone is disruptive when what they're doing interrupts the gathering, scares people, or keeps going after a polite request to stop. It can come from anger, emotional distress, alcohol or drugs, mental illness, or a family conflict.

    Use the threat levels to size it up:

    • Level 1, curious but calm: not disruptive. Be friendly and offer help.
    • Level 2, rule breaker: address it respectfully and set limits.
    • Level 3, verbally disruptive: loud, arguing, won't stop. Ask them to step aside and talk.
    • Level 4, threatening or aggressive: keep your distance, call for backup, be ready to call LEO.
    • Level 5, actively dangerous: protect others and call LEO.
    • Level 6, active assailant: use the active shooter steps.

    On the radio, a disruptive person is Code Orange. See threat levels

    What do I do first?
    • Radio Code Orange on Channel 9 and ask for backup before you step in.
    • Walk up calm and confident, not aggressive.
    • Stand 3 to 6 feet away. Don't touch them, and don't box them in.
    • Speak low and slow. Ask open questions and really listen.
    • Then give short, simple directions, one at a time.

    If there's any hostility, use two ABs: one talks and one watches and protects (contact and cover). Follow ABC: the Servant Leader alerts, an AB defuses it quietly, and LEO steps in if it escalates. Open the ABC SOP

    What do I actually say?
    • Open: “Hi, I'm ___. I want to help. What happened today?”
    • Give choices: “Here are two options that work for you…”
    • Set a boundary: name the behavior, the limit, and what happens next. For example: “I need you to lower your voice. If you can't, I'll need you to step outside with me.”

    Don't say “Calm down” or “What's your problem?” and don't match their volume. If they insult you, don't take the bait. Bring it back to solving the problem.

    When do I call the police?

    Call the LEO on duty, or 911, when:

    • It escalates after you've tried to calm it down.
    • They're threatening or aggressive and won't back off.
    • Anyone is in danger, or they get physical.
    • You see or suspect a weapon (Code Black: call 911).
    • Someone who has been trespassed comes back, or a person refuses to leave.
    Can I put my hands on someone?

    Only if it's necessary, you're trained, and it's justified. Think of force as a staircase you can go up or down: presence, then words, soft control, hard control, defensive tools, and deadly force last. Use the lowest step that works, and step down as soon as they comply.

    Force must be necessary, reasonable, and proportionate. Never use force because someone insults you or won't leave. That's a police matter. Deadly force is only for an immediate threat of death or serious harm.

    What if they won't leave?

    Don't drag anyone out. Keep talking, keep your distance, and call the LEO on duty. Law enforcement can escort them off the property. After review, leadership may issue a no-trespass order.

    What do I write down afterward?

    File an incident report: the time and place, what happened, who was involved, what you did, and who you told. Then debrief with your team lead.

    Domestic violence and family matters

    From the Sheepdog Safety Member study guide and Safety Drills Manual, plus Georgia state hotlines. Check with AB leadership on anything your team handles differently.

    Someone runs in saying a person is after them. What do I do?
    • Get between them. Keep the person who's afraid behind you and the other person in front of you.
    • Radio for backup and move the person to a safe, private spot away from the crowd, with an AB staying with them.
    • Talk to the other person calmly and ask them to leave. Never send the two out together if the person doesn't want to go.
    • If there's a threat, a weapon, or they won't leave: call 911, lock the doors (Code White lockout), and keep people away from doors and windows.
    • Tell your team lead so the pastor knows.
    What if I see a couple fighting in the parking lot?

    Radio it to the team right away and watch from a safe distance. Don't walk into it alone. If it turns physical or you see a weapon, call 911, lock the doors, and protect the person being targeted.

    What if someone has a protective order against another person?

    We don't enforce court orders. Police do. If the restrained person shows up, keep the two apart, don't let them into the building, alert your team lead, and call the LEO on duty or 911.

    If a member tells you they have a protective order, ask them to share it with AB leadership ahead of time so the team knows who to watch for.

    What about custody disputes, or a parent who isn't allowed to pick up?

    It's not our job to decide custody. Our job is to keep kids safe and follow the check-in system: only an authorized person picks up a child.

    If someone who isn't allowed demands to see or take a child, stay calm, don't let them into the kids' area or sanctuary, keep the child with their guardian or Formation Kids staff, call for backup, and call LEO if they won't leave. Let leadership and police handle custody questions.

    What if I think a child is being abused?

    You don't need proof. Reasonable suspicion is enough. Report it to the state first, then tell leadership. Don't question the child, don't wait to gather more, and don't confront the parents. Keep it private.

    Georgia DFCS (24/7): 1-855-GACHILD (1-855-422-4453). If a child is in danger right now, call 911.

    What if I think an older or disabled adult is being abused?

    Report it yourself, based on what you saw. You don't need anyone's permission.

    Georgia Adult Protective Services: 1-866-552-4464 (press 3), Monday to Friday, 8am to 7pm. If they're in danger right now, call 911.

    How do I help someone who's being abused at home?

    Listen, believe them, and keep it private. Don't confront the abuser or try to fix it yourself. Connect them with a pastor for care, and share the National Domestic Violence Hotline: 1-800-799-7233, or text START to 88788. If they're in danger right now, call 911.
